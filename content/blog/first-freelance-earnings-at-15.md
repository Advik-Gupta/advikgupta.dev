## What earning at 15 taught me

1. **Deadlines are real.** "I'll do it tomorrow" stops working when someone else's client is waiting.
2. **Clear instructions save everyone time.** The tasks that went best had a design and a written list of what was needed. The ones that went badly were "just make it look good." I later learned this lesson the hard way, as an adult, with [a client who never knew what they wanted](/blog/bad-freelance-client-lessons).
3. **Reliability beats brilliance.** My friend kept giving me work because I delivered when I said I would, not because my code was clever.
4. **Under-pricing is normal at first, but don't stay there.** Early work is partly paid learning. Raise your rates as your work proves itself.
5. **Your portfolio starts with your first paid project.** Ask permission to show it.

## How to start freelancing as a teenager (my honest advice)

- **Build three real things first.** A personal site, a clone of a site you like, and something for a real person (family business, school club).
- **Work through someone you know.** Small agencies, older friends, local businesses. Trust matters more than platforms at the start.
- **Involve your parents.** For payments, safety and agreements. It's a strength, not a weakness.
- **Write down what you're doing for whom.** Even a WhatsApp message listing the tasks and price counts as a basic agreement.
- **Don't overpromise.** Say "I'll check and confirm by tonight" instead of "yes, easy."
- **Keep learning fundamentals.** AI makes the first draft easy, and fundamentals make it correct. ([Here's how I learned before AI](/blog/learning-to-code-young-before-ai).)
